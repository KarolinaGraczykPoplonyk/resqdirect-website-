## Introduction

Can you tow an electric car when it breaks down? Does it need a flatbed recovery truck, and is recovering an EV more complicated than recovering a petrol or diesel vehicle?

Electric and hybrid cars are increasingly common on British roads, but their recovery requirements are not always the same as those of conventional vehicles.

An electric car may break down because of a puncture, a flat 12V battery, a charging fault or a problem with its electrical system. Many breakdowns can be resolved at the roadside. However, when the vehicle cannot continue its journey, the method used to move it matters.

This guide explains what UK drivers should know about electric vehicle recovery, using official safety guidance and recent breakdown statistics.

## 1. Are Electric Cars More Difficult to Repair at the Roadside?

Electric vehicles are sometimes assumed to be harder to repair after a breakdown. Recent AA figures provide a more interesting picture.

According to the AA's UK EV Readiness Index for the third quarter of 2026:

- **88.1% of electric vehicle callouts** were resolved at the roadside.
- **83.5% of petrol vehicle callouts** were resolved at the roadside.
- Only **1.3% of EV callouts** involved vehicles running out of charge.

These are figures from breakdowns attended by AA patrols, not a measure of how often every electric or petrol car breaks down.

## 2. Running Out of Charge: How Common Is It Really?

Range anxiety remains one of the most familiar concerns associated with electric vehicles.

Yet the proportion of AA EV breakdown callouts caused by running out of charge has fallen considerably.

In 2015, running out of charge accounted for **8.3% of EV callouts**. By the third quarter of 2026, that figure was **1.3%**.

## 3. What Causes Electric Cars to Break Down?

Electric vehicles have fewer conventional engine components than petrol and diesel cars, but they still rely on tyres, suspension, electrical controls and other mechanical systems.

Some breakdown causes are shared across vehicle types, while others are specific to the powertrain.

| Breakdown problem | Electric car | Petrol or diesel car |
|---|---|---|
| Flat or damaged tyre | Yes | Yes |
| Wheel or suspension damage | Yes | Yes |
| Low-voltage battery failure | Yes | Yes |
| Running out of energy | Depleted traction battery | Empty fuel tank |
| Charging system fault | Yes | Not applicable to conventional petrol/diesel cars |
| Conventional engine failure | No combustion engine in a battery EV | Possible |
| Electric drivetrain fault | Possible | Depends on vehicle type |

A puncture may be repairable at the roadside. A vehicle with locked wheels, damaged suspension or a serious electrical fault may need to be transported.

The important question is what has happened to the vehicle and whether it can be moved safely.

## 4. Can You Tow an Electric Car With a Rope?

**Conventional towing with an electric vehicle's wheels rotating on the road is generally not recommended.**

The Department for Transport explains that rotating the wheels can generate electrical current through the electric motor, potentially damaging the vehicle's high-voltage systems.

This is why a recovery method suitable for one petrol car may not be suitable for an EV.

The recommended transport methods generally include a flatbed recovery vehicle, a suitable transport trailer or appropriately used wheel dollies.

The manufacturer's instructions must always be checked.

### Comparing electric vehicle recovery methods

| Recovery method | What drivers should know |
|---|---|
| Flatbed recovery truck | Generally recommended for EV transport, subject to the vehicle's condition and loading requirements |
| Vehicle transport trailer | Can be suitable when the equipment is correctly rated and the manufacturer's instructions are followed |
| Wheel lift with dollies | May be appropriate where the relevant wheels are supported |
| Conventional tow rope | Generally unsuitable for normal EV towing |
| Very short movement from danger | May be permitted under strict conditions in government guidance |

The Department for Transport allows limited movement of an electric or hybrid vehicle out of immediate danger, such as a live traffic lane, provided specified conditions are met.

This is not permission to tow the vehicle along the road to a garage.

## 5. What Is Tow Mode?

Some electric cars have a feature called Tow Mode, Transport Mode or a similar name.

This can cause confusion because the function does not necessarily mean the vehicle can be towed normally.

On certain models, it is intended to allow controlled movement while the vehicle is loaded onto recovery equipment.

For example, Tesla's Model 3 recovery instructions describe Tow Mode as part of the transport preparation process.

Other manufacturers use different procedures.

A vehicle may also require a functioning low-voltage electrical system to release its parking brake or enter the appropriate mode.

**Selecting neutral or activating Tow Mode does not automatically make an EV suitable for conventional towing.**

## 6. Why Can a Flat 12V Battery Immobilise an Electric Car?

Electric cars usually contain a high-voltage battery for propulsion and a separate low-voltage electrical system.

The low-voltage system supports important vehicle functions, which may include locks, control electronics and the systems required to prepare the car for movement.

If that system fails, an EV can become unresponsive even when its main traction battery still contains charge.

This can complicate recovery because the vehicle may be unable to release its parking brake or enter the required loading mode.

A recovery operator may need additional equipment or a manufacturer-approved procedure.

Drivers should explain whether the vehicle powers on, whether its wheels can move and whether any electrical warnings are displayed.

## 7. Why Does Electric Vehicle Weight Matter?

Vehicle weight is important when planning any recovery or transport job.

Some electric vehicles are heavier than similarly sized petrol models because of their traction batteries.

However, weight varies significantly between models, so it would be misleading to assume that every EV is heavier than every conventional car.

Before loading a vehicle, the operator needs to consider its actual weight and dimensions, alongside the capacity of the recovery truck, trailer and towing vehicle.

This is particularly important for larger electric SUVs and heavier vehicles.

The Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency publishes guidance covering recovery vehicle weights and operating requirements.

A vehicle's size and loading condition can also affect the equipment needed.

## 8. Are Hybrid Cars Different?

Hybrid cars combine a combustion engine with an electrical drive system, but their designs vary.

Conventional hybrids, plug-in hybrids and mild hybrids do not necessarily have identical recovery requirements.

Some have electric motors connected to their drivetrains and high-voltage components that require additional precautions.

A hybrid should therefore not automatically be treated like a conventional petrol car when arranging towing.

The safest approach is to identify the exact make and model and follow its manufacturer's recovery instructions.

## 9. Recovering an Accident-Damaged Electric Vehicle

An electric vehicle involved in a collision may require additional precautions, especially if its high-voltage battery or electrical components have been damaged.

The Department for Transport identifies several potential hazards, including electrical shock, chemical exposure and battery fire.

Warning signs can include:

- Smoke or unusual vapour.
- Hissing, popping or unexpected noises.
- Chemical smells.
- Visible damage near the battery.
- Signs of overheating.

Not every damaged EV has an unstable battery. Minor damage away from high-voltage components may allow normal recovery procedures.

However, a seriously damaged vehicle can require specialist assessment.

If there are signs of fire, smoke or battery instability, keep away from the vehicle and contact the emergency services.

Never touch exposed orange high-voltage cables or attempt to inspect damaged battery components yourself.

## 11. Does Electric Car Recovery Cost More?

Not necessarily.

There is no single UK recovery price that applies to every electric vehicle, and an EV is not automatically more expensive to recover simply because it is electric.

The cost of a recovery or transport job can depend on:

- Collection and delivery locations.
- Distance.
- Vehicle size and weight.
- Whether the vehicle rolls and steers.
- Accident damage.
- Access restrictions.
- Additional loading equipment required.

An undamaged electric car that can be loaded normally presents a different situation from one with locked wheels or significant collision damage.

Accurate vehicle details help the operator assess the job and provide a suitable quotation.

## 12. What Should You Tell the Recovery Operator?

Before arranging electric or hybrid vehicle recovery, have the following information ready:

1. Vehicle make, model and year.
2. Whether it is fully electric or hybrid.
3. Exact collection location or postcode.
4. Delivery location.
5. Whether the vehicle powers on.
6. Whether the wheels roll and the steering operates.
7. Whether the parking brake releases.
8. Whether the vehicle has been involved in a collision.
9. Any battery warnings, unusual smells, smoke or visible damage.

Photographs may also be useful if the vehicle has accident damage or unusual loading requirements.

The more accurate the information, the easier it is to assess the recovery arrangements before collection.

## 13. Frequently Asked Questions

### Can an electric car be transported on a trailer?

Yes. A suitable vehicle transport trailer is one of the methods recommended in Department for Transport guidance, provided the vehicle's loading requirements and equipment limits are followed.

### Can you tow a Tesla?

Tesla publishes model-specific transport instructions. For the Model 3, flatbed transport is recommended, and Tow Mode is intended for controlled movement rather than unrestricted towing along the road.

### Do electric cars frequently run out of charge?

AA data for Q3 2026 indicates that running out of charge accounted for only 1.3% of its EV breakdown callouts.

### Can an EV with a flat 12V battery be recovered?

Potentially, but the failure may prevent normal loading functions from operating. Manufacturer-approved procedures and suitable equipment may be needed.

### Can a hybrid car be towed?

It depends on the specific vehicle and drivetrain. Always follow the manufacturer's instructions.

### Is electric vehicle recovery dangerous?

Most routine EV recoveries can be carried out using appropriate equipment and procedures. However, damaged high-voltage components or unstable batteries may introduce additional hazards requiring specialist assessment.

## 14. The Simple Rule to Remember

An electric car is not necessarily harder to repair at the roadside, but it may require a different method of transport when recovery becomes necessary.

**Identify the vehicle → check its condition → follow manufacturer guidance → arrange suitable recovery equipment.**

The safest recovery method depends on the particular vehicle, not simply whether it is electric.
